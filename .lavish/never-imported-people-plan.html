Why another step is needed

01

Already in the CRM

A Person exists, but an update is held because its stage or agent mapping is missing. The deployed mapping repair handles this.

02

Never made it into the CRM

The original creation was held. There is no Person to update. We need proof that creating one now is safe, plus a permanent link back to the old hold.

An old “skipped” result stays skipped. A new recovery result explains that the Person was created later. We preserve both parts of the story.

What you will do

How we avoid duplicates

Use the source identity

We use the Organization, FUB account and exact Person ID. Two people sharing an email address are not automatically merged.

Check the old successes

If the Person was already imported, we show that result. A missing identity entry cannot override proof of a previous successful import.

Respect deletion markers

Deleted, missing or inconsistent former targets stay held. Recovery cannot resurrect a deleted Person or adopt a similar-looking record.

If a worker crashes halfway

A transaction is a group of database writes that succeeds together or rolls back together. We use one for each Person.

Before the transaction finishes

No partial Person is committed. When the worker’s claim expires, another worker can retry the same item.

After the transaction finishes

The next worker finds the saved identity and result. It recognizes the completed work instead of creating the Person again.

Cancellation stops future items. Successful People remain. A fresh preview can resume only the unfinished eligible remainder; an old worker’s expired claim cannot authorize a late write.

Then bring the rest across

The recovered group becomes eligible for the existing import workflows. Each still needs its own source evidence, preview and confirmation.

  1. Tags and custom fieldsUse the qualified recovery snapshot or a later one. Keep catalog and option mappings explicit.
  2. Notes and tasksKeep existing author, task type and timezone checks. Respect local edits and deleted records.
  3. Historical activityUse a separately qualified history capture taken after the recovery’s core snapshot. If it is missing, show that prerequisite.
  4. Later core updatesRecognize the recovered Person’s approved mappings and starting values. Local edits remain protected.

Core recovery is not “migration complete.” The screen will show which families are not started, partial, held or completed.

The implementation plan

  1. Freeze the recovery rules and contractsExplicitly distinguish recovery from ordinary new-Person admission and from updating an existing Person.
  2. Extend the existing admission workerAdd held-record proof, saved mappings, safe creation, retry and cancellation. No additional polling loop or pod.
  3. Connect the existing family workflowsTeach their identity checks and selectors to recognize genuine recovery results. Preserve their source and permission rules.
  4. Add the administrator experienceFind holds → save choices → full preview → confirm → results and follow-on coverage.
  5. Verify before releaseTest duplicate races, crashes, stale workers, erased identities, changing mappings, tenant isolation, partial cancellation and every follow-on family.

What stays outside this slice

Automatic contact merging, restoring deleted People, arbitrary unsupported source records, silent FUB fetching, ongoing synchronization, workspace activation and production Kubernetes rollout remain separate.

Source snapshots and old evidence

A snapshot is saved source data captured over a time interval. Recovery uses one qualified retained report at the current admission boundary or later. It will not quietly mix old and new captures, use an obsolete boundary, or make a source request for you.

Why this plan needs a contract review

Normal admission currently excludes original source records and uses original mapping approvals. Recovery needs a new explicit mode, its own initial approvals, honest provenance, updated family qualification and a compatibility fence for older software. These changes are proposed here; the recovery feature has not been implemented.
